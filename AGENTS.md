# AI conventions

## About this repository
Public graduate-business-course portfolio of Niko Bonilla (Nicholas Bonilla), a Finance & International Business student at the Shidler College of Business, University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa.
Canonical file: AGENTS.md. CLAUDE.md points here.

## My field
Finance and international business (BBA candidate, Shidler College of Business). My applied background is property and casualty insurance (an internship rotating through underwriting, claims and other units), the music business (a recording artist signed to AWAL under Sony Music Entertainment, with merchandise sold online), and student organization governance. Use examples from finance, international business, insurance and the music business when they fit.

## Where things are
- capabilities/<capability>/  one folder per capability, each with README.md, spec.md and the model file
- docs/briefs/          written BEFORE work: scope + hypothesis
- docs/decisions/       written AFTER work: recommendations
- analysis/             findings and figures
- data/                 sourced inputs, with provenance
- RESUME.md             my resume; do not change what it says

## Naming
- The directory matters most. A file in the wrong folder is harder to find. If you are not certain which folder a file belongs in, ask me
  before you write it — do not choose for me.
- Graded files use the exact filename the stage brief gives — lowercase,
  hyphens, no spaces. Dated documents are YYYY-MM-DD-slug-type.md (no name — the repo is yours);
  the stage page says so when they do.
- Slugs name the engagement, never the week, the course, or the assignment
  number.
- Never invent a path or a filename. I will give you the exact one.

## How I work
- Explain concepts fully and walk the worked example. Do not hand me conclusions.
- Show the arithmetic, name the units and currency, and state the assumptions behind any financial figure so I can check each step.
- Critique my reasoning directly. I would rather be corrected than agreed with.
- When you are uncertain, say so and say what would resolve it.

## What you may and may not draft
- You MAY explain, critique, debug, quiz me, and draft mechanical files.
- You MAY NOT write my briefs, analyses, memos, or reflections.
- Every statistic or figure you give me is a draft until I verify it against a source.

## Documentation
When work changes, update the document that describes it in the same commit.
A capability's README names the engagements that exercised it — keep that current.

## Scope
Do the work I asked for. If you notice something worth doing that I did not ask
for, tell me instead of doing it.

## Commits
Descriptive messages: what changed and why. Never "update" or "stuff".

## Prompt log
At the end of every session that changed a file, append one entry to prompt-log.md: the date, what I asked, what you produced, what was wrong and how it was caught. Never backfill earlier sessions and never edit a past entry.

## Never include
No credentials, no API keys, no personal data about anyone, no licensed or
copyrighted material. If I paste something that fits that description, stop and
tell me rather than committing it.

## Never paste into a model
This repository is public and anything pasted into a model is treated as publication. Never paste, upload or describe in identifiable detail:
- First Insurance Company of Hawaiʻi: policyholder or claimant records, underwriting files, claims files, loss runs, pricing or rating information, internal reports, and the materials, drafts and findings for the cross-departmental presentation to company leaders; names or details of the professionals I shadowed.
- AWAL / Sony Music Entertainment: my recording, distribution or publishing contracts, royalty and payout statements, unreleased music and lyrics, and non-public analytics from Spotify for Artists or other streaming dashboards.
- Merchandise business: Shopify customer, order or payment records, including names, addresses and emails.
- University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa Student Housing Services: any resident, student or parent information, such as names, housing assignments, emails, phone calls and faxed documents. I handled these under strict confidentiality.
- Delta Sigma Pi, Rho Chi Chapter: member rosters and contact details, votes, disciplinary or conflict-resolution matters, internal minutes, and bylaws or national materials not already public.
- PACE, Business Executive Society of Tomorrow and other organizations: member, participant, donor, scholarship-application or partner records.
- Pennsauken Athletic Department: team rosters and photographs identifying students.
- Anything under a confidentiality agreement with an employer, and my own phone number, home address, student ID, or financial and account details.
If a document would not be safe in a public repository, it is not safe in a chat window either.
